# What Tokovo Can Make

Tokovo turns stories into cinematic episodes that unfold across phones: messages, feeds, calls,
notifications, keyboards, lockscreens, recordings, and multiple devices. Creators can revise the
story, cinematography, language, cast, or platform cut without rebuilding the episode by hand.

The phone is not an overlay. It is the stage on which the story happens.

## Content Formats

### Serialized phone-native drama

Build recurring 30–60 second episodes with stable characters, relationships, locations, and
cliffhangers.

**Example:** A new manager joins a chaotic team group. Every episode reveals another private chat,
calendar conflict, or message sent to the wrong channel.

### Topical fiction

Let an original fictional cast react to a match, product launch, cultural moment, or developing
public story while it is still relevant.

**Example:** Four friends watch a championship final from different cities. Their group chat,
private messages, notifications, and public posts reveal that one of them already knows the result.

Topical reconstructions must distinguish authored dramatization from authentic screenshots or
evidence. Characters from an existing television or film property require appropriate rights;
original universes and clearly lawful parody are safer foundations for a repeatable product.

### Family, workplace, and relationship drama

Stage family groups, office politics, founder conflicts, college friendships, dating, breakups,
weddings, roommate disputes, and generational misunderstandings.

**Example:** Someone announces an engagement in the family group while two relatives privately try
to stop the announcement from reaching one specific person.

### Mystery, thriller, and investigation

Use deleted messages, unknown callers, location drops, conflicting posts, screen recordings,
privacy settings, and multiple-device perspectives as narrative evidence.

**Example:** A journalist receives a location pin. The audience sees a second phone deleting the
message before the journalist opens it.

### Comedy and satire

Tell stories about corporate absurdity, autocorrect disasters, influencer behavior, family-chat
rituals, fake productivity, fandom, and internet culture.

**Example:** A team spends the entire night preparing a “minor wording change” that the founder
replaces with an emoji five seconds before launch.

### Story-native promotion

Place a product, link, booking, recommendation, order, event, or launch inside a conversation where
it changes the plot. The commercial moment should earn its place by creating desire, conflict,
proof, or payoff.

**Example:** A friend shares a restaurant booking link to repair an argument. The reservation
confirmation becomes the episode's emotional reveal rather than a detached advertisement.

### Creator commentary and visual essays

Let a creator narrate an argument while posts, chats, searches, notifications, and calls visualize
the people and evidence around it.

**Example:** A creator explains how a rumor spread by showing the original private message, the
cropped repost, the notification spike, and the correction reaching fewer people.

### News reconstruction and explainers

Reconstruct how a public event moved across digital surfaces, or explain a complex subject through
the people affected by it.

**Example:** An operations team responds to an outage across alerts, a work channel, customer
messages, and public updates.

These episodes must label simulations clearly and must not present generated phone screens as
authentic evidence.

### Education, training, and public-interest stories

Teach cybersecurity, scam awareness, finance, workplace behavior, history, language, or social
skills through decisions and consequences rather than a slide deck.

**Example:** A suspicious delivery message moves from notification to family chat to banking alert,
showing exactly where the character could have stopped the scam.

### Multi-perspective anthology

Show one event from several phones. Each device can reveal information that the other characters do
not have.

**Example:** A resignation appears as courage on the employee's phone, betrayal on the manager's
phone, opportunity on a recruiter's phone, and gossip in the family group.

### Branches, alternate endings, and personalized editions

Keep the same story structure while changing a decision, character, sponsor, region, ending, or
camera plan.

**Example:** One cut ends when the character sends the message; another ends when they delete it;
a third follows the recipient's phone after it arrives.

### Live-event and fandom companion stories

Create pre-match, release-night, backstage, award-show, or fandom episodes that run alongside a
larger cultural event.

**Example:** A music release is experienced through an artist's private group, a fan account, a
reviewer's notifications, and a surprise direct message.

### Documentary-style digital-life stories

Reconstruct a friendship, startup, investigation, relationship, campaign, or public controversy
through its digital traces.

**Example:** A company launch is retold from the first private idea through the failed demo, the
team's recovery, and the first customer notification.

## Multilingual and Regional Editions

Tokovo separates story semantics from platform presentation, allowing one episode structure to be
adapted into regional editions. A serious localization pass can change:

- dialogue, captions, names, idioms, and cultural references;
- left-to-right or right-to-left layout;
- keyboard script and input behavior;
- app and system chrome;
- typography and text metrics;
- voice, music, and sound cues;
- line lengths, pacing, and camera framing;
- platform, device, and visual appearance.

Current implementation depth varies by package. Device system surfaces cover English, Hindi,
Arabic, and Japanese; X covers English, Arabic RTL, and Hindi; WhatsApp covers English and Arabic
RTL. Tokovo should not claim universal language fidelity until the selected app, keyboard, font,
voice, and layout combination has passed visual review.

**Example:** The same workplace episode can ship as an English iPhone cut, an Arabic RTL edition,
and a Hindi Android edition. Localization may change the joke, message length, typing rhythm,
notification copy, voice, and shot framing while preserving the narrative beats.

## Production Capabilities

One episode can declare:

- one phone, split-screen phones, picture-in-picture, or a multi-device stage;
- WhatsApp, X, Instagram, iMessage, LinkedIn, Snapchat, Teams, Typewriter, and OS surfaces where
  their current package contracts support the scene;
- chats, feeds, DMs, profiles, calls, typing, notifications, lock/home screens, and screen
  recording;
- semantic camera targets, cuts, reframing, focus, dolly, tracking, lens effects, filters, and
  multi-output compositions;
- music, procedural sound, app/OS cues, voice tracks, captions, backgrounds, and overlays;
- alternate camera plans and platform cuts over the same logical story;
- deterministic preview, validation, MP4 rendering, posters, metadata, logs, and diagnostics.

The same authored input and frame resolve the same logical world. That makes revisions,
localization, alternate cuts, and recurring series reviewable instead of becoming one-off editing
projects.

## Best Initial Product Wedges

Tokovo's strongest initial product categories are:

1. recurring phone-native drama series;
2. topical original fiction responding quickly to culture and public events;
3. story-native branded content that viewers choose to watch.

They combine emotional hooks, repeatable characters, multilingual editions, fast iteration, and a
clear path to publishing or monetization.
